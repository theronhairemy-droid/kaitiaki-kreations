📌📌 Today's Message: The Prisoner That Refused to Stay Captive 🌺

Mona Parsons was born in Middleton, Nova Scotia, in 1901, and by every rule of her time she was supposed to live a quiet, decorated life. 🌺 She trained in elocution in Wolfville and Boston, chased the stage to New York, and danced as a chorus girl in the Ziegfeld Follies in 1929. 💃 Then she trained as a nurse, met Dutch businessman Willem Leonhardt, married him in 1937, and settled into a wealthy life in the Netherlands. 🇳🇱 She had money, status, and, when the war came, an arranged way out: her husband had secured her safe passage home to Canada. She refused it. 🚫

When Germany invaded in May 1940, Mona and Willem quietly joined a Dutch resistance network that hid downed Allied airmen and moved them toward escape. 🛩️ Airmen slept in the servants' quarters and hid in a secret closet in her bedroom during Nazi inspections, then slipped away toward fishing boats and British submarines. 🌊 The network was infiltrated and betrayed. Willem got warning and slipped away; Mona stayed in the house to stall the Gestapo and protect it, and on September 29, 1941, they took her. 🚨 On December 22, 1941, a Nazi military tribunal found her guilty of treason and sentenced her to death by firing squad, making her the only Canadian female civilian imprisoned by the Nazis. She appealed with such composure that the sentence was commuted to hard labour for life. ⚖️

For nearly four years she lived the part nobody puts on a poster: prisons and labour camps, hunger, disease, forced work in hospitals, on railway lines, and at an airfield under Allied bombing, even quietly miswiring weapons in a munitions job. 💣 Then, on March 24, 1945, Allied bombs hit near Vechta Prison, and in the chaos Mona walked out with a young Dutch baroness, Wendelien van Boetzelaer. 🏃 The two posed as German sisters and walked for three weeks, trading labour for food, sleeping in barns, dodging patrols. They were separated near the border, and Mona kept going alone on infected feet, down to 85 pounds, until a Dutch farmer helped her reach Canadian troops, who turned out to be the North Nova Scotia Highlanders, soldiers from her own home province. 🇨🇦

She came home to commendations from General Dwight Eisenhower and RAF Air Chief Marshal Lord Tedder, was reunited with Willem, and eventually settled back in Wolfville, where she died in 1976, largely forgotten by the country she had refused to run back to. 🏡 Canada Post finally put her on a stamp in 2023, nearly fifty years after her death. 💮 Wednesday is Remembrance Day, and we rightly honour the people in uniform. Mona's story asks us to widen the frame a little: remembrance also belongs to a Nova Scotia chorus girl who had a ticket home, tore it up in her heart, hid strangers in her closet, faced a firing-squad sentence standing straight, and then walked 125 miles out of the dark on ruined feet. 🌹 If her name is new to you, say it once out loud today, and tell one other person. That is how the forgotten get remembered. Forward is always a choice, and that means YOU too!

🛠️🌿 The Deep Dive: 5 Things Most Pro Landscapers Won't Tell You 🌿🛠️

1. Plant Container Trees and Shrubs in the Chinook Window 🌳
Most people think planting season ended with the leaves, but roots keep growing until the soil itself drops to about 4°C, and Edmonton soil at root depth is still workable through a week like this one, with highs of 7 to 11°C and no snow in the forecast. A container tree or shrub planted now, flare at grade and watered in deeply, settles its root ball and starts rooting into the surrounding clay-loam months before a spring planting even wakes up. The catch is water discipline: keep soaking it on every mild day until the ground actually freezes. A dry root ball going into winter is the number one quiet killer of fall plantings.

2. Hunt Girdling Roots While the Canopy Is Bare 🔍
November is the one month you can see a tree's whole skeleton, and the flare tells the truth the leaves hide. Look for roots that cross over the trunk flare or circle it at soil level, especially on maples and lindens planted from containers, where the roots learned to spin in the pot. A stem-girdling root acts like a slow tourniquet: the trunk thickens, the root does not move, and the tree strangles itself over five to ten years. Surface girdlers can be cut cleanly now with a sharp chisel and mallet at the point where they meet the flare; deep or trunk-fused ones are an arborist call, and either way, finding it in year three instead of year nine is the difference between a five-minute cut and a removal bill.

3. Read the Wear Line on Single-Stage Blower Paddles ❄️
A single-stage snowblower clears to pavement with rubber paddles, and those paddles are a wear part with a built-in confession: most have a wear-indicator hole or line moulded into the rubber, and when the wear reaches it, the paddles no longer touch the ground. From then on the machine leaves a thin skin of snow and ice on every pass, which is exactly the layer clients then pay you to salt all winter. Swapping paddles and the scraper bar takes about twenty minutes on a bench in a warm garage, and this mild week is when to do it. Check the paddles before the first storm writes the review for you.

4. Dry-Fit the Plow Before the First Dispatch 🚜
Every plow setup has a first-storm failure hiding in it, and it is almost never the blade: it is a corroded mount pin that will not seat, a lift chain worn to half its links, or hydraulic fluid gone milky, which means water got in and will freeze in the lines at -15°C. Mount the plow on a dry day like today, run it through full lift, angle, and drop twenty times, grease every pivot, and check that the headlights and harness connectors are clean and tight. Thirty minutes in November daylight buys you the one thing you cannot get at 4am in a blizzard: a truck that answers. Log what you replaced on a card in the glove box so next fall's check takes five minutes.

5. Move Deciduous Shrubs Leaf-Off, Before the Ground Locks 🌿
A deciduous shrub that has dropped its leaves has shut down its water demand, which makes the next two weeks the lowest-shock transplant window of the whole year in Edmonton, as long as the soil still digs. Dig wide rather than deep, keep the root ball intact and damp, and replant at the exact same depth it grew at, then water it in once, deeply, so soil settles against the roots with no air pockets. Do not fertilize and do not prune the top to "balance" it; the roots need every stored carbohydrate for winter. The shrub sits dormant, heals its cut roots at the first spring thaw, and leafs out in its new home like it never moved.

🇨🇦 Canadian Track'O'The Day 🎶

Innocent - Our Lady Peace (clean). Toronto's Our Lady Peace, built around Raine Maida's unmistakable voice, were already arena-sized in Canada when they made Gravity in 2002 with producer Bob Rock, and "Innocent" is that record's reflective heart, a song looking back at the kid you were before life got its hands on you. For me it lands perfectly in Remembrance week, because half of remembrance is exactly that: measuring who we became against who we set out to be. Play it on the morning drive, windows up, coffee down, and let it set the tone before the tools come out. 🚗

🤣 Today's Musical Funny-Bone 🦴

Witch Doctor - David Seville (clean). Before he was David Seville, he was Ross Bagdasarian Sr., an Armenian-American songwriter who in 1958 recorded himself singing nonsense syllables, then sped the tape up until his voice turned into a squeaking witch doctor. "Witch Doctor" went to No. 1, and the same tape-speed trick invented Alvin and the Chipmunks later that year, which means this one goofy single quietly launched a franchise older than most of our parents. Play it when the crew needs morale in its purest, dumbest form: sixty-plus years old and still impossible to hear without grinning. 😊

🤘 Hair Whippin', Head Bangin', Finger Blazin' Therapy 🔥

Final Resistance - Dark Tranquillity (clean). Gothenburg, Sweden gave the world melodic death metal, and Dark Tranquillity, formed in 1989, are one of the three bands that built the sound from nothing. "Final Resistance" opens Damage Done (2002) at full sprint: twin-guitar harmony lines over blast-speed drums, Mikael Stanne barking like the end is scheduled and he has read the itinerary. This is the therapy slot doing its job, controlled fury with a melody you can hum while your neck recovers. Play it loud on the last job of the day and finish the list like you mean it. 🤘

🎤 Tongue Trippin', Bar Spittin, Beat Killin Bars 🔥

Grindin' - NF ft. Marty (clean). NF is Nate Feuerstein out of Gladwin, Michigan, a rapper who built a stadium career in the clean lane, all intensity and no profanity, and "Grindin'" from Therapy Session (2016) is his work-ethic manifesto with Marty of Social Club Misfits riding shotgun. A year later his album Perception debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200, which is what grinding in obscurity eventually pays when you refuse to quit. For my money this is tailor-made for the 6am version of me, boots half-laced, talking himself into the day. Play it before the first shovel hits the ground. 🥾

🇳🇿 Māori Track / Aotearoa Pick 🌿

Cool Me Down - The Black Seeds (clean; filed honestly as an Aotearoa pick). Wellington's Black Seeds have been New Zealand's reggae and dub institution since 1998, a big live band built for summer fields, and "Cool Me Down" from On the Sun (2004) is their sound in one track: deep bass, patient horns, and a groove that lowers your heart rate on contact. Auckland is getting rained on all week while Edmonton gets sun, so consider this a care package sent in the other direction for once. Play it on the drive home with the heater on and pretend, briefly, that you are somewhere warmer. ☀️

🌟 Ron Hairemy Spotlight ✨

Remember Everything - Five Finger Death Punch (clean). Las Vegas's Five Finger Death Punch are usually the heaviest hand in the room, but the standout on American Capitalist (2011) is this ballad, Ivan Moody singing straight at a childhood full of things a kid should never have had to carry, and at the strange mercy of remembering all of it. In Remembrance week, of all weeks, a song about carrying memory honestly, the good filed next to the unbearable, feels less like a playlist pick and more like the whole point of the next two minutes of silence. Some things we remember because we must, and we carry them forward so the people coming up behind us carry a little less. That's the goal. That's LAWNgevity. 🌺

Mona Parsons had a ticket home and refused it, and that one refusal is still echoing eighty years later, which tells you what a single stubborn, decent decision is worth. 🌹 Your action hook today is Tip 2: before the snow flies, walk your trees with bare branches and actually look at the flare, because the girdling root you find this week is the tree you do not have to remove in 2031. Shoutout to every crew still closing out fall lists in a hoodie in November, and to the North Nova Scotia Highlanders, who were exactly where a homesick prisoner needed them to be.
