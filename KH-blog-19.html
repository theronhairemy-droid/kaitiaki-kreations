📌📌 Today's Message: The Maze That Refused to Hold Him: William Kurelek 🎨

William Kurelek was born March 3, 1927, on a grain farm near Whitford, Alberta, the oldest of seven children in a Ukrainian immigrant family, and he grew up on the prairies in the middle of the Great Depression. 🌾🇺🇦 The farm failed and the family moved to a dairy farm near Stonewall, Manitoba, and young Bill's love of drawing was not encouraged by hard-working parents who wanted a practical son. He studied at the Ontario College of Art and at the Instituto Allende in Mexico, but mostly he taught himself from books, and by his mid-twenties he was living in England, alone, broke, and falling apart inside. 🎨😔

In 1952, suffering from clinical depression and emotional problems, he admitted himself to the Maudsley Psychiatric Hospital in London, where he was treated for schizophrenia, and in that hospital he painted the picture that would define him. 🖼️💙 "The Maze" (1953) shows his own skull split open, its chambers laid bare, and his soul lying in the centre like an exhausted rat that cannot find its way out of the maze of its own mind. He worked with Edward Adamson, a pioneer of art therapy, at Netherne Hospital, and the painting became one of the most famous images of mental illness ever made. 🧠🌀

But Kurelek did not stay in the maze. He came back to Canada, converted to Roman Catholicism in 1957, and poured everything he had into the work, painting the prairies and the Ukrainian pioneers he grew up with, a 160-painting series on the Passion of Christ, and the Nativity imagined in Canadian settings like an igloo and a trapper's cabin. 📖✨ He wrote and illustrated "A Prairie Boy's Winter" (1973) and "A Prairie Boy's Summer" (1975), both award winners, illustrated W.O. Mitchell's "Who Has Seen The Wind", and in 1976 he was made a Member of the Order of Canada. His paintings now hang in the Museum of Modern Art, the National Gallery of Canada, and the Art Gallery of Ontario. 🏆🇨🇦

Kurelek died of cancer in Toronto on November 3, 1977, at just fifty years old, but the maze never held him, and here is the wildest part of the story: his painting became the cover of Van Halen's "Fair Warning" in 1981, so a whole generation of metalheads carried his tortured mind home on vinyl. 🤘🎸 If you are carrying a maze in your own head today, make something out of it, a dot, a line, a song, a conversation, because the thing you build from the hard part might be the thing that outlives all of it. Forward is always a choice, and that means YOU too!

🛠️🌿 The Deep Dive: 5 Things Most Pro Landscapers Won't Tell You 🌿🛠️

1. Silicone-Spray the Snowblower Chute and Auger Housing Before the First Wet Snow ❄️
Wet snow sticks to bare steel like glue, and once the chute starts packing up, the auger loads up, the belt slips, and you are out there clearing a clog with a stick in the middle of a route. A quick coat of silicone spray inside the chute and across the auger housing before the first wet fall makes the snow slide straight through instead of bonding, and a five-minute job now saves a dozen frozen clog-clearings this winter. Re-spray after every few heavy uses, and do it on dry metal so it actually bonds.

2. Match the De-Icer to the Surface and the Temperature 🧂
Rock salt is cheap and it works, but only down to about -9°C on the pavement, and it chews concrete, pits pavers, and burns every bed it melts into. Magnesium chloride melts colder and is kinder to concrete, calcium magnesium acetate is the one to reach for near plantings and new concrete because it barely corrodes at all, and plain sand is the honest answer when the pavement is colder than any melter can handle. Pick the product for the surface you are standing on and the temperature under your boots, not just whatever bag is closest, and never put any melter on concrete poured this year.

3. Re-Crown Gravel Drives and Walks Now While the Ground Is Still Soft 🛤️
A flat gravel drive holds every chinook melt in a puddle, and that puddle freezes into a rut that swallows tires and trips boots for the rest of the winter. While the ground is still soft, rake and re-grade the drive to a gentle two percent crown so meltwater sheets off to the edges instead of pooling in the wheel tracks, and tamp it firm so the first freeze locks the shape in. It is an hour with a rake now, or a season of ice ruts later.

4. Fit a Freeze-Relief Tee on the Sump Discharge Where It Daylights 💧
The buried sump line freezes first right where it daylights, and when it ices shut, the pump has nowhere to send the water, so the next melt backs up toward the foundation. A freeze-relief tee fitted on the discharge just inside the daylight point gives the pump an escape route: if the buried run ices over, the water dumps out of the tee above ground instead of flooding the basement. It is a ten-dollar fitting and twenty minutes, and it turns a January emergency into a puddle you can see.

5. Carry a Spare Auger Belt and the Socket That Opens the Belt Cover 🔧
Auger belts glaze and snap without warning, usually mid-route on the coldest push of the week, and the shop will not have your size when every other blower in the city is down too. A spare belt in the truck, plus the exact socket that opens the belt cover, turns a lost route into a ten-minute roadside swap, so check the belt number now, buy the spare while the shelves are full, and tape the socket to the belt so they never get separated.

🇨🇦 Canadian Track'O'The Day 🎶

Hand in My Pocket - Alanis Morissette (clean). Ottawa's own Alanis brings the second single off Jagged Little Pill (1995), written with Glen Ballard, a song built out of contradictions, broke but happy, poor but kind, one hand in her pocket and the other giving a high five. The album went on to win five Grammys including Album of the Year, and this track is the sound of holding all of it at once and grinning anyway. Play it when the day is all contradictions at once and you are holding them all anyway.

🤣 Today's Musical Funny-Bone 🦴

Fryin' Bacon Nekkid - Roger Alan Wade (clean). Country storyteller Roger Alan Wade brings the comedy track with the greatest hook ever written about a kitchen, "Lovin' you is like fryin' bacon nekkid," a love song where the loving hurts just as much as the grease burns. Written by D. Knutson and Wade, it is pure front-porch funny, the kind of song that gets funnier every time the pan pops. Play it when the morning needs a laugh and the kitchen needs a warning label.

🤘 Hair Whippin', Head Bangin', Finger Blazin' Therapy 🔥

Rasputin - Turisas (clean). Hämeenlinna, Finland's battle metal crew take Boney M's 1978 disco classic about the mad Russian mystic and run it through accordion, violin, and a full metal charge, turning a dancefloor filler into a war chant. The 2007 single is the sound of a band that refuses to take the joke seriously and plays it harder because of it. Play it when the cold is biting and the only answer is volume.

🎤 Tongue Trippin', Bar Spittin, Beat Killin Bars 🔥

No Vaseline - Ice Cube [E] (explicit language throughout). The closing track of Death Certificate (1991), and one of the most feared diss records ever made: Cube, fresh out of N.W.A over a money dispute, takes aim at his former group and their manager Jerry Heller with no padding and no mercy, every bar landing exactly where he meant it to. Thirty-five years on it is still the benchmark for what a diss track is supposed to do. Play it when the bars need to cut clean and leave no doubt who won.

🇳🇿 Māori Track / Aotearoa Pick 🌿

Taniwha (feat. Randy Blythe) - Alien Weaponry (clean). Waipu, Northland's Alien Weaponry, brothers Lewis and Henry de Jong with bassist Tūranga Morgan-Edmonds, bring the third single off Te Rā (2025), and this one is the real thing, a Māori band singing their own guardian spirit in te reo Māori, with Lamb of God's Randy Blythe roaring through the bridge. The track was written entirely in te reo before producer Josh Wilbur helped shape an English verse for Randy, and it landed March 26, 2025 as one of the heaviest things the band has ever cut. A taniwha is the guardian I just finished in dots at 54 hours, so this one hits home twice. Play it when the guardian in you needs to stand up.
