📌📌 Today's Message: The Magician Who Refused to Believe in Luck 🎩

Some people spend their whole lives afraid of a date on the calendar. 🗓️ James Randi spent his proving the date had no power at all. He was born Randall James Hamilton Zwinge in Toronto on August 7, 1928, the eldest of three kids, and his whole story starts with a verdict he refused to accept. As a boy he was hit by a car while riding his bicycle, and he spent thirteen months in a body cast while doctors told him he would never walk again. 🚲 He read conjuring books flat on his back in that cast, fell in love with Harry Blackstone Sr., and walked out of it anyway, on his own two feet, with a deck of cards in his hands. 🃏 At fifteen he exposed a local preacher who claimed to read minds, and got hauled off to jail for four hours when the preacher's wife called the police. At seventeen he dropped out of high school and joined a travelling carnival as a conjurer and escape artist, and The Amazing Randi was born. 🎪

The carnival kid became the most famous magician in the world, and then he did something nobody expected: he turned the tricks back on the people who used them to lie. 🎩 In 1956 he spent 104 minutes in a sealed metal coffin sunk in a hotel swimming pool on live television, breaking Harry Houdini's 93-minute record. In 1972 he publicly challenged Uri Geller, the spoon-bending psychic the whole world was swooning over, and showed the world it was standard stage magic, not a superpower. 🥄 Geller sued him for fifteen million dollars and lost, and the court ordered Geller to pay a hundred and twenty thousand for filing a frivolous case. Randi co-founded the Committee for the Scientific Investigation of Claims of the Paranormal in 1976, won a MacArthur "genius" Fellowship in 1986, and used it to expose television faith healer Peter Popoff, whose "divine revelations" were coming through a hidden earpiece from his wife backstage. 📺

Then he built the dare that made him immortal. 💰 In 1996 he founded the James Randi Educational Foundation and put a million dollars of his own foundation's money on the table: the One Million Dollar Paranormal Challenge, a standing offer to anyone who could demonstrate a paranormal power under test conditions both sides agreed to. Hundreds of people tried, psychics, dowsers, healers, spoon-benders, every flavour of the supernatural, and nobody ever took a cent. 🚫 He even ran Project Alpha, a three-year hoax where he planted two young magicians inside a university psychic research lab and watched the scientists certify their tricks as real, just to prove how badly people want to believe. The challenge stayed open until 2015, and the million dollars stayed exactly where it was. 🏆

Randi died on October 20, 2020, at ninety-two, and he left behind a world that is a little harder to fool than the one he was born into. 🕯️ That is what refusing looks like when you do it for a lifetime: not a single dramatic moment, but a thousand small ones, every single time somebody tried to sell fear or wonder as a miracle, and one Toronto kid in a body cast said prove it. On a Friday the 13th, that is the whole sermon. 🖤 Luck is not a force, it is a story somebody tells about a date, and you are allowed to tell a better one. Port Dover understood that in 1981, and tonight forty-five years of riders prove it again. So knock on wood if it makes you smile, walk under the ladder if it makes you laugh, and meet whatever is waiting for you this week with your eyes open and your feet moving. Forward is always a choice, and that means YOU too!

🛠️🌿 The Deep Dive: 5 Things Most Pro Landscapers Won't Tell You 🌿🛠️

1. Fog-Freezing Black Ice: Sand the Walks Before the Fog Lifts 🧊
This morning's fog advisory runs until 12:42 PM, and fog is not just low cloud, it is moisture settling on every cold surface in the city. When the temperature is hovering around zero, that moisture freezes into a clear skin on walks and steps that looks like nothing more than a damp patch, and it is the slickest ice of the season because nobody sees it coming. Sand or grit the shaded north steps and any walk that never sees the sun before the fog lifts, not after somebody goes down on it. Rock salt will melt it, but on a damp surface at these temperatures the sand is what keeps feet under people right now.

2. Cold-Crank Test the Truck and Blower Batteries Before Tuesday's -7°C 🔋
Cold is a battery test you cannot fake, and Tuesday night drops to -7°C, which is cold enough to expose every weak battery on the crew. A battery that spins fine at 10°C can lose a third of its cranking power in the cold, so get a load test done now, most parts stores do it free, and clean the terminals while you are there, because a green crust on a post is a no-start waiting for the coldest morning of the week. If the blower has an electric start, charge that battery too, and if the truck cranks slow even once this weekend, replace the battery on your schedule, not on Tuesday night's.

3. Foam-Sleeve the Exposed Water Lines in the Garage Before the Cold Snap 🧣
Any water line running through an unheated garage, shed, or crawlspace is one cold night away from splitting, and Tuesday's -7°C is the night to beat. Foam pipe sleeves cost a few dollars a length, slide on in minutes, and buy you the margin that keeps a copper or PEX line alive when the temperature drops past freezing. Cover every exposed run, especially near exterior walls and the line feeding the outdoor tap, and if the outdoor tap has an interior shutoff valve, close it and open the outside tap to drain the line completely. A burst pipe does not announce itself until the first thaw, when it floods the garage while you are out on a route.

4. Keep Snow Piles Off the Foundation Wall 🧱
When the snow starts piling up, keep it off the foundation wall, because a pile against the house melts against the footing all winter long. That meltwater feeds the weeping tile, works its way into every hairline crack, and freeze-thaw cycles it wider a fraction at a time, all season. Pile on the lawn side instead, at least a metre clear of the wall, and let the grass take the melt, because turf bounces back in spring and a footing does not. Put the pile zone in the route notes now, before the first real dump, so the crew is not deciding it in a blizzard.

5. Pull the Drip Lines Out of the Raised Beds Before the Freeze 💧
Thin-wall drip tape and emitter lines are the first irrigation casualty of the freeze, because any water left inside them expands and splits the walls at the first hard frost. Pull the lines out of the raised beds now, drain them, coil them, and store them indoors, and give them a quick puff of air from the compressor if you have one handy. Mice chew the fittings all winter if you leave them out, and a spring reinstall takes twenty minutes, while replacing a chewed, split system takes a whole morning and a trip to the supply yard.

🇨🇦 Canadian Track'O'The Day 🎶

Try Honesty - Billy Talent (clean). The Toronto four-piece formed in 1993 as Pezz, and this was their 2003 debut single, the one that put them on the map straight out of the self-titled album, all jagged guitars and a demand for straight answers in a relationship gone sideways. It lands on the shelf today for a reason, because James Randi spent ninety-two years asking the exact same question of every psychic, healer, and spoon-bender who crossed his path: try honesty. Play it on the drive to the first job, when you need your head straight and your bullshit detector warmed up.

🤣 Today's Musical Funny-Bone 🦴

Gin and Juice - Hayseed Dixie [E] (explicit lyrics throughout). The Tennessee rockgrass crew formed in 2000 around frontman John Wheeler, Barley Scotch to his friends, and built a whole career out of running rock and rap classics through banjo, mandolin, and fiddle, starting with their 2001 debut, A Hillbilly Tribute to AC/DC, which sold more than a hundred thousand copies. Here they take Snoop Dogg's 1994 classic Gin and Juice and give it the full hillbilly treatment, and the joke lands because they play it completely straight, like it was always a front-porch song. Play it when the crew needs a laugh that doubles as a history lesson.

🤘 Hair Whippin', Head Bangin', Finger Blazin' Therapy 🔥

Varyags Of Miklagaard - Amon Amarth (clean). From Twilight of the Thunder God (2008), this one tells the story of the Varangian Guard, the Norse warriors who sailed to Miklagaard, the Vikings' name for Constantinople, and served as the Byzantine emperor's elite bodyguard, the hardest men in the hardest city on earth. It is all galloping riffs and Johan Hegg roaring about oaths sworn in a foreign hall, and it is exactly the right weight for a foggy Friday the 13th. Play it when the fog rolls in and the day needs a shield wall.

🎤 Tongue Trippin', Bar Spittin, Beat Killin Bars 🔥

Man Don't Dance - Big Shaq (clean). Michael Dapaah is a London comedian who built the fictional drill rapper Big Shaq in his mockumentary series SWIL, and Man's Not Hot went viral in 2017, hit number three in the UK, and went platinum. This is the sequel, released in May 2018, where Shaq insists he does not dance, the beat disagrees with him for three solid minutes, and the whole thing peaked at number seventy-eight in the UK anyway, because the joke was too good to quit. Play it on the last drive home, when the day is done and the crew is still laughing.

🇳🇿 Māori Track / Aotearoa Pick 🌿

April Sun in Cuba - Dragon (clean). Dragon formed in Ponsonby, Auckland in 1972, moved to Sydney in 1975, and this was the lead single from their fourth album, Running Free, released in October 1977 and written by Marc Hunter and Paul Hewson. It spent twenty-two weeks on the Australian chart, peaking at number two, hit number nine at home in New Zealand, and has since gone five-times platinum in NZ with more than eighty million Spotify streams, and the band is marking fifty years of it with a national tour next April and May. Filed honestly as an Aotearoa pick, Kiwi-born and Australian-based, and still the sound of a warm afternoon. Play it when the sun finally shows up and the week deserves a chorus.
