📌📌 Today's Message: The Champion Who Refused to Hide the Hurt 🚴‍♀️

Clara Hughes was a Winnipeg kid who started speed skating at sixteen and picked up cycling at seventeen, and by Atlanta 1996 she was standing on an Olympic podium twice, two bronze medals in road cycling around her neck. 🚴‍♀️🥉 Most athletes would have called that a career. Clara was just getting started, and the hardest part of her story had nothing to do with medals at all. 🏅

After Atlanta, the darkness moved in. 😔 Depression settled over her so heavy it nearly ended everything she had built, and for a long time she carried it the way so many of us do, in silence, smiling for the cameras while the inside fell apart. 💔 But Clara did something most people never manage: she came back. At twenty-eight, an age when most elite athletes are winding down, she laced up her skates again, and by Turin 2006 she was Olympic champion in the 5,000 metres, the first Canadian woman ever to win that gold. 🥇⛸️ She added a team pursuit silver in Turin and a bronze in Vancouver 2010, where she also carried the Canadian flag, and finished with six Olympic medals, the only athlete in history to win multiple medals at both the Summer and Winter Games. 🇨🇦✨

Then she made the bravest move of her whole career, and it had nothing to do with a finish line. In 2010 she went public about her depression and became the founding spokesperson for Bell Let's Talk, putting her own name on a conversation Canada was still too scared to have. 🗣️💙 And in the spring of 2014 she got on a bicycle and rode it, all of it: Clara's Big Ride, 110 days, 12,000 kilometres, 105 communities, 235 events, coast to coast to coast, from Toronto to Ottawa on Canada Day, telling her story in town after town and listening while strangers told her theirs. 🚲🗺️ She wrote it all down in her memoir, Open Heart, Open Mind, and she has never stopped saying the thing that needs saying. 📖❤️

Clara's ride is still going, because every time one of us talks about the hard stuff instead of hiding it, that ride gets a little longer. 🌅 So here is the ask, crew: check on your people, say the real thing out loud, and ride your own version of the Big Ride however you can, one honest conversation at a time. 🤝🌿 Forward is always a choice, and that means YOU too!

🛠️🌿 The Deep Dive: 5 Things Most Pro Landscapers Won't Tell You 🌿🛠️

1. Keep a Roof Rake on the Truck Before the Snow Piles Up 🏠
After a heavy fall, pull the snow off the lower roof edge from the ground and you stop ice dams from forming at the eave, which is where the real damage starts. Never climb a snowy roof to do it, work in sections with a plastic blade so you don't chew up the shingles, and offer it as a client add-on the day the first real dump lands, because every house with a low eave needs it and almost nobody thinks to ask.

2. Test the Block Heater and Its Cord Before the Minus-Twenties 🔌
Plug in on a cold morning and feel for warmth at the block after thirty minutes, or check it with a tester light if you want to be sure. A dead cord at -25°C is a no-start and a lost day, and the shop won't have a spare when every other truck in the city is dead too, so carry a spare cord in the cab right now while it's still cheap and easy.

3. Swap the Washer Fluid to -40°C Rated Before the First Freeze 💧
Summer fluid freezes solid in the lines and cracks the reservoir and the pump on the first -10°C night, and that's a repair bill for a five-dollar jug you meant to swap. Do it now while the day is still above freezing, and keep a spare jug in the cab, because a smeared windshield on a dark winter road is not a place you want to be guessing.

4. Get the Winter Tires On While the Shops Still Have Slots 🛞
All-season rubber hardens below 7°C and loses grip right when you need it most, while winter tires stay flexible in the cold and actually bite the road. The swap done this week beats the queue after the first real dump, when every shop in Edmonton is booked solid and you're sliding through intersections on hockey pucks.

5. Pack the Truck's Winter Emergency Kit Now, Not in a Ditch 🧣
Blanket, candle and lighter, high-calorie snacks, tow strap, folding shovel, spare gloves, phone charger. A truck stuck in a drift on a rural route at -20°C is a survival situation, not an inconvenience, and the kit you pack on a mild Wednesday is the kit that gets you home on a cold one.

🇨🇦 Canadian Track'O'The Day 🎶

Still Waiting - Sum 41 (clean). Ajax, Ontario's own Sum 41 bring the pop-punk fire with the lead single from Does This Look Infected? (2002), written in the shadow of 9/11 and still one of the sharpest rants the genre ever produced. Deryck Whibley and the boys built a career on hooks that hit like a hammer and choruses you can't unhear, and this one is the sound of a generation refusing to sit still. Play it when the truck is loaded, the day is young, and you need the volume up and the excuses down.

🤣 Today's Musical Funny-Bone 🦴

Yakety Sax - Boots Randolph (clean). From 1963, written with James Q. "Spider" Rich, and it is the sound of every chase scene ever filmed, the Benny Hill Show theme that turned a saxophone into a punchline. One listen and you're picturing yourself sprinting through the yard in fast motion, arms flailing, while the whole crew cracks up. Play it when the day needs a laugh break, or when somebody on site is doing something so chaotic it deserves its own soundtrack.

🤘 Hair Whippin', Head Bangin', Finger Blazin' Therapy 🔥

Living Dead Beat - Children Of Bodom [E] (explicit per the Spotify flag on the full studio version). Finnish melodic death metal legends Children Of Bodom bring this one off Hate Crew Deathroll (2003), Alexi Laiho's guitar work slicing through the frost like a chainsaw through January ice. It is every bit as feral as the title promises, and it is the track for when the cold is biting and you need something heavier than the weather to keep moving. Play it when the shovel work gets grim and the only answer is volume.

🎤 Tongue Trippin', Bar Spittin, Beat Killin Bars 🔥

Onion Head - Sean Price [E] (explicit language throughout). Brooklyn MC Sean Price, Heltah Skeltah and Boot Camp Clik royalty, delivers this one off Jesus Price Supastar (2007), featuring Tek of Smif-N-Wessun, with bars so dense they need a second listen just to catch the bodies. It is underground hip hop at its most surgical, and not a wasted syllable in it. Play it when the work is done, the truck is parked, and you want bars that demand your full attention.

🇳🇿 Māori Track / Aotearoa Pick 🌿

So True - The Black Seeds (clean). Wellington reggae and dub outfit The Black Seeds bring this one off Into the Dojo (2006), all warm horns, rolling bass, and that easy groove that makes a grey November afternoon feel like a beach day. Filed honestly as an Aotearoa pick, the Black Seeds are a Pākehā-led Wellington band, not a Māori act, but their sound is pure New Zealand sunshine and it fits this slot like a glove. Play it when the sun breaks through and the work starts feeling like a holiday.

🌟 Ron Hairemy Spotlight ✨

Black Rose Immortal - Opeth (clean). My pick, and the only right choice after Clara Hughes: Opeth, my all-time favourite band, always has been and always will be, with twenty minutes and fourteen seconds of Mikael Åkerfeldt's genius off Morningrise (1996), a song that starts in a whisper and ends somewhere past the horizon. Åkerfeldt is a god of the night song, and this is the track that proves it, acoustic passages and death metal storms woven into one long journey that refuses to hurry and refuses to shrink. Clara rode 12,000 kilometres one pedal stroke at a time, and this song does the same thing with twenty minutes of music: it takes the long road, it earns every minute, and it arrives exactly where it meant to go.
