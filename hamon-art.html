Hamon Art

The makers of the Hamon whānau — art and music in the blood, from the ngahere of Aotearoa to Edmonton.

One line, many hands

Art didn’t start with me, and it doesn’t end with me. I come from a line of makers: a great-granduncle who spent a lifetime rendering the New Zealand bush one dot at a time, his son who keeps that legacy in galleries today, and a generation of cousins painting, carving, and making music on the far side of the Pacific. My own pointillism — every dot of it — descends from this line. This page is a signpost to the whānau: follow the links and see the work where it lives. The full line is written out in my whakapapa.

Ike (Isaac) Hamon

Son of Albert Awanui Hamon, grandson of Rei. Ike stands in the Expansion — the generation that carried the Hamon spark out into the world, through heritage work and production. The name travels because this generation carried it.

Nate Hamon

The Hamon ear, turned to sound. Nate’s music rides under the Mayan Fox name — atmospheric, far-reaching stuff, the dots traded for waves. His “Mighty Wonders” has already been spotlighted in Kaitiaki’s LAWNgevity picks.

Francis Hamon

He carries his grandfather’s full name — Francis Rei Paul Hamon. A painter and muralist working Māori motifs — tiki, koru, manaia — across canvas and spray paint, and a competitor on Auckland’s Art Battle circuit. Kaitiaki’s second cousin; the two only found each other in 2026, through this very site.

Huia Hamon

Daughter of Awanui, granddaughter of Rei — and a musician. Huia’s track “Waka Huia” was Kaitiaki’s Kiwi Track o’ the Day in his favourite blog post: a cousin’s voice carrying home across the Pacific.

Albert Awanui Hamon

Rei’s son, and the keeper of the line. A working multi-disciplinary artist — realism, surrealism, carving, murals — and the man behind the Rei Hamon Gallery and Hamon Art NZ, carrying his father’s exhibitions across Aotearoa and Australia. In te reo his name is Arapata: the ancestor’s name, worn in its English form.

Rei Hamon

Francis Rei Paul Hamon (1919–2008). The root of everything on this page: the renowned Māori pointillist who spent a lifetime rendering the New Zealand bush dot by dot. Kaitiaki’s great-granduncle — and the reason the Edmonton branch of this whānau makes its art one dot at a time.
