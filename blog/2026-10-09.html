🌟 Today's Message: The Band That Bet on Itself

I've been thinking about three kids from Willowdale, Toronto 🇨🇦 — Alex Lifeson, Geddy Lee, and a drummer named John Rutsey — who started a band called Rush in 1968 and spent six years playing bars and high school dances for almost nobody 🎸😅. Then in 1974 the drum chair changed hands, and in walked Neil Peart from Hamilton — a bookish kid who'd gone to London to make it, failed, and come home with his tail between his legs 🥁📚. The band was blown away by his playing — and the man could WRITE, so he became their lyricist too ✍️🔥. Fly By Night went platinum, but then their third record stiffed so hard the label started sharpening the axe 📉😬. Mercury Records wanted radio singles. The band wanted something else entirely.

So they made the most stubborn decision in Canadian rock history 💪🇨🇦. Instead of playing it safe, Rush recorded 2112 — a 20-minute sci-fi epic about a future where music itself is outlawed and one man finds a guitar 🚫🎸. The label braced for a career-ending flop… and it went triple platinum 🏆🏆🏆. Then came Permanent Waves and Moving Pictures — The Spirit of Radio, Tom Sawyer, Limelight, YYZ 📻🔥. Three guys. No image consultants, no focus groups, no compromise — just the three of them getting better at being THEM, louder, for forty years 🥁🎸🎤. Tens of millions of records later, they'd become the most beloved cult band on the planet 🌍💛.

And then life hit that drummer harder than anyone should ever be hit 💔. In 1997, Neil's daughter Selena, just 19, was killed in a car crash. Less than a year later, his wife Jacqueline died of cancer 😔🕊️. Neil told Geddy and Alex he was DONE — retired, finished — and then he did the most Neil Peart thing imaginable: he climbed on a motorcycle and rode clear across North and Central America, alone with his grief, for thousands of miles 🏍️🌎. He wrote it all down in Ghost Rider: Travels on the Healing Road 📖 — and mile by mile, the music came back. He remarried, rejoined the band in 2001, and Rush roared back for another decade and a half 🔥🥁.

What's left behind is staggering: Officers of the Order of Canada in 1996 🍁🎖️, the Canadian Music Hall of Fame in 1994, the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2013 — and the same three men for 41 straight years, no replacements, no reunion circus, no drama 🤝. Neil left us on January 7, 2020, after a private three-year fight with brain cancer that he never made anybody else carry 🕊️🥁. And this year, Geddy and Alex announced the Fifty Something tour — with Neil's family's blessing — to take these songs back out on the road one more time 🥹🎸. So here's my Thanksgiving weekend homework for you: put on Tom Sawyer, call somebody you've been meaning to call, and tell them what they mean to you before the turkey hits the table 📞🦃.

And remember, forward is ALWAYS a choice, and that means YOU TOO! 💛🚀

🛠️🌿 The Deep Dive: 5 Things Most Pro Landscapers Won't Tell You

1. Deep-water your trees before the freeze. Here's the one nobody believes until they've replaced a dead spruce: most winter tree death in Edmonton isn't cold — it's drought. Trees head into winter dry, the frozen ground locks up what little moisture is left, and the wind strips the rest out of the needles and branches all season. Before the ground freezes solid, give every tree a long, slow, deep drink — several hours on a soaker hose at the drip line, not a five-minute sprinkle. A hydrated tree is a tree that wakes up in April.

2. Blow out the irrigation lines before Saturday. A charged irrigation line meeting a hard freeze is a pipe bomb with a timer on it. Water expands, PVC and backflow preventers crack, and you don't find out until spring startup — when the bill shows up. Today, while it's still above zero, run the compressor through every zone and drain the backflow. Saturday's snow doesn't care about your schedule, so beat it to the punch.

3. Disconnect and drain the garden hoses tonight. This one costs people real money every single year. A hose left attached and full of water freezes backward through the spigot and bursts the pipe inside the wall — and that burst doesn't announce itself until the spring thaw floods the basement. Tonight: shut the water, disconnect every hose, drain it, coil it, hang it up. Two minutes a hose. Cheapest insurance in the whole blog.

4. Don't prune the spring bloomers in fall. Put the pruners away from the lilacs, the forsythia, the mock-orange. Spring-flowering shrubs set their flower buds in late summer on the wood that's already there — prune them now and you've just cut off next spring's entire show. The rule is simple: spring bloomers get pruned right after they bloom, never before. Shape them in June, thank yourself in May.

5. Cut out black knot while the branches are bare. Black knot — those ugly black tar-like swellings on Edmonton cherry and plum trees — spreads its spores in the spring rain. Dormant season, when the tree is bare and you can actually see every infected branch, is when you hunt it. Cut well below the knot — 15 to 20 cm into clean wood — disinfect your blade between cuts, and burn or bin what comes off. Never compost it. An hour of dormant detective work now saves the whole tree later.
