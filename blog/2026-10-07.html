🌟 Today's Message: The Seat She Refused To Leave

On November 8, 1946, a Black businesswoman from Halifax walked into the Roseland Theatre in New Glasgow, Nova Scotia, while her car was being repaired — and changed Canadian history by accident 🇨🇦🎬. Viola Desmond hadn't set out to be an activist that night ✊. She was a successful beautician who'd built her own salon and beauty school after being shut out of training in Halifax because of her race 💪, and she'd simply bought a ticket for a movie 🎟️. But when she sat in the main-floor seats instead of the balcony where Black patrons were expected to sit, the theatre demanded she move — and she refused 🚫.

What happened next was swift and brutal 😤. She was dragged out by police 🚔, jailed overnight, and convicted — not of defying segregation, because no law on the books actually required it, but of dodging a one-cent amusement tax, the difference between the balcony seat she'd paid for and the floor seat she'd sat in 🪙⚖️. The system couldn't punish her for being Black in the wrong seat, so it punished her for a penny. She paid a $26 fine she didn't owe 💸, and the conviction followed her for the rest of her life. She died in 1965, never pardoned, never apologized to 🕊️.

But here's the part that matters: she didn't let it end there 🔥. Viola poured what was left of her life into fighting the injustice, and her case became one of the sparks of Canada's civil rights movement ✊🇨🇦. Decades later, the reckoning came — in 2010 Nova Scotia granted her the first posthumous free pardon in Canadian history and formally apologized 📜, and in 2018 she became the first Canadian woman to appear alone on a Canadian banknote, her face on the $10 bill 💵👑. From a one-cent tax charge to the ten-dollar bill — that's what refusing to move can build 🏆.

So the next time something feels too big, too fixed, too "that's just how it is" — remember a woman who sat down in a theatre seat and wouldn't get up 🪑💪. She didn't have a movement behind her. She didn't have a plan 📋❌. She had a seat, a spine, and a refusal to accept what was wrong 🌱🔥. Change doesn't always start with a crowd 👥. Sometimes it starts with one person, in one seat, on one ordinary night, deciding: not this time 🌟.

…forward is always a choice, and that means YOU too! 💛🚀

🛠️🌿 The Deep Dive: 5 Things Most Pro Landscapers Won't Tell You

1. Cut the wire basket off your B&B trees. That wire basket around a balled-and-burlapped tree is for shipping, not planting — but most crews bury the whole thing. In Edmonton clay, the basket doesn't rust away fast enough, and as the trunk thickens the wires strangle it from the outside in. Cut away the top half of the basket and peel the burlap back from the top third of the root ball before backfilling. Five minutes with bolt cutters now saves a slow ten-year death later.

2. Mulch volcanoes are tree killers. Piling mulch against the trunk looks tidy and kills trees — it rots the bark, hides the root flare, and gives voles a cozy winter condo with room service. Keep mulch in a wide flat donut: 2–3 inches deep, pulled back a full 3 inches from the trunk so you can see the flare. If you can't see where the trunk widens at the base, the mulch is too deep or too close. Fix it now before the snow locks it in.

3. Gypsum doesn't fix clay — stop buying it. The garden centre will happily sell you bags of gypsum for "breaking up clay," and it feels like doing something. It isn't. Gypsum only helps sodic (high-sodium) soils, which yours almost certainly isn't — for plain heavy clay, the calcium does nothing to the structure. What actually opens clay up is organic matter: compost, compost, compost. Skip the gypsum aisle and put that money toward a yard of compost instead.

4. Core aerate this week — it's your last window. Soil is still warm enough for recovery, and Saturday's snow is your deadline. Pull cores now, leave the plugs on the surface to break down, and broadcast seed directly into the holes — the seed-to-soil contact in an aeration hole beats any broadcast spreader on flat ground. Water it in before Friday's rain does the job for you. Skip this and you're waiting until next fall.

5. Leave the perennials standing. Put the pruners down — ornamental grasses, sedum, and coneflower heads earn their keep all winter. The standing foliage insulates the crown against freeze-thaw, feeds the birds, and looks incredible with frost on it. Only cut back the stuff that turns to mush: hostas, daylily foliage, anything already slimy. Tidiness is a spring job; right now, messy is protective.

🎶 Ron Hairemy's Songs That Guarantee LAWNgevity

We understand that landscapes are timeless, and deserve every bit of time and care — just like the classics that keep us motivated through the day. When long hours on the job, the right music makes all the difference. These are the tracks that keep us moving, focused, and locked in.
No Nickelback. No Slipknot. All Music. No filler. (Except for Comedy… Everybody needs a good laugh!)
So without further ado, here's some dope jams!

🇨🇦 Canadian Track'O'The Day: Ordinary Day – Great Big Sea 🔥🎸🇨🇦💥😎
Explicit? No. Clean as a whistle.
Straight out of St. John's, Newfoundland, Great Big Sea spent two decades turning East Coast folk into arena-sized singalongs, and Ordinary Day is their signature — the song about finding the extraordinary hiding inside the routine. It's the sound of rolling up your sleeves and deciding today counts. Best listened to at 7am with the truck warming up and the whole day still unwritten ☀️🚜.

😂 Today's Musical Funny-Bone: Canadian Idiot – "Weird Al" Yankovic 🤘🤣🔥💀🎤
Explicit? No. Just deeply, lovingly offensive to no one in particular.
The undisputed king of the parody takes Green Day's American Idiot and aims it north of the border — beer, hockey, donuts, "aboot," and a preemptive strike against the politest nation on Earth, all in two and a half minutes. For me, this one's personal: Weird Al was on repeat all through growing up, Alapalooza was the first CD I ever bought, and when I caught Al live at Capital Ex (Klondike Days), the man opened the show with this very song 🎪🎸. Best listened to when the crew needs a laugh and the day needs deflating 😂🇨🇦.

🧠🔥 Hair Whipping, Head Banging, Finger Blazing Therapy: Painkiller – Judas Priest 🌫️🎸🖤🌪️⚔️
Explicit? No. Just loud.
The 1990 title track that redefined what heavy metal could be — double-kick drums like artillery, twin guitars at full sprint, and Rob Halford hitting notes that shouldn't be physically possible. Painkiller is thirty-plus years old and still the ultimate "one more hour" song. Best listened to when the job is fighting back and you need to fight harder 💪🔥.

🎤 Tongue Trippin', Bar Spittin, Beat Killin Bars: Kin (I'm Outside) – Prof (with Pete & Bas and JonRay) 🧠💥🎶🔥✍️
Explicit? Yes — profanity throughout.
Minneapolis rapper Prof teams up with UK duo Pete & Bas and JonRay for a full-throttle heater — rapid-fire bars, relentless bounce, zero brakes. And this one's personal: it's the track I start my landscaping day with every single morning — boots on, truck warming up, volume up 🚜🔥. The official "let's go earn it" anthem.

🌿 Māori Track: What's the Time Mr. Wolf – New Zealand Singers 🪶🔥🌿🇳🇿
Explicit? No. It's a playground song, whānau.
The old Kiwi classic — the call-and-response game every New Zealand kid grew up playing, given full singalong treatment on 2012's Songs of New Zealand. Simple, joyful, and woven into the culture deep enough that every Kiwi knows every word. Best listened to when you need reminding that the best things are the simple ones done together 🎶🤙.

🌾 Ron Hairemy Spotlight: Don't Stop Me Now – Queen 🎹👑🔥🌟💫
Explicit? No. Pure joy, no filter needed.
Pulled from your "All time faves in the spotlight!" playlist — Freddie Mercury's 1978 rocket ship off the Jazz album, and quite possibly the most unstoppable three minutes ever recorded. Piano, thunder, and a man having the time of his life daring you to keep up. It's the spiritual sequel to everything the spotlight stands for: full speed, no brakes, no apologies. Best listened to at full volume on the way to a job you're about to crush 🚀🎶.
