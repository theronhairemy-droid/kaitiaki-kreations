🌟 Today's Message: Bette Midler and Hulaween: The Queen of Halloween Who Gave New York Its Gardens Back

Every October, one woman owns Halloween harder than anybody alive 🎃👑. Bette Midler, born in Honolulu, Broadway legend, Grammy and Emmy and Tony winner, put on the buck teeth and the towering red wig in 1993 and gave the world Winifred Sanderson in Hocus Pocus 🧹✨. The movie was a modest thing on release and then grew, year after year, into the defining Halloween film of a generation, and Midler did not just star in it: she leaned all the way in, showing up in full costume, year after year, as the host of the spookiest party in New York City 🎭🕯️. For me, that is the first lesson of her story: if you are going to own a season, own it in costume, in public, with your whole chest.

But here is what she saw when the party lights were off 😔🌃. Midler moved back to New York in the mid 1990s and found the green heart of the city trashed and neglected: parks littered and crumbling, and more than a hundred community gardens, built by hand in the neighbourhoods that needed them most, sitting on the city's auction list 🌧️🥀. These were not rich people's gardens. They were the vegetable plots, shade trees, and breathing room of underserved blocks across all five boroughs, and they were about to be sold out from under the people who had grown them 🌱💔. A superstar could have cut a cheque, posed for a photo, and gone home. Midler looked at those gardens and decided she was not going home.

So in 1995 she founded the New York Restoration Project, NYRP, and went to work with her sleeves rolled up 💪🌳. When the city moved to auction off 114 community gardens in 1999, NYRP and the Trust for Public Land stepped up and bought them, saving the whole network in one swing 🏡✊. Since then NYRP has planted more than 65,000 trees, restored parks, and built and protected green space across all five boroughs, deliberately focused on the neighbourhoods with the least green and the most need 🌲🏙️. And she funded the fight the way only Bette Midler would: with a Halloween party. Hulaween, NYRP's annual costume gala running since about 1996, is her flagship fundraiser: the 2022 edition raised over $2 million in a single night, the 2017 gala filled the Cathedral of St. John the Divine, and the October 24, 2025 gala marked NYRP's 30th anniversary and honoured Midler herself 🎃💃. She hosts it in full costume every year, in 2025 dressed as her own 1973 album cover, because of course she did.

Thirty years on, the joke has become a skyline 🌆🌿. Kids who were not born when Winifred Sanderson first lit the black flame candle now play in gardens that exist because the Queen of Halloween refused to let her city rot 🕯️❤️. That is the part I keep coming back to, crew: she took the thing she was famous for loving, the costumes, the camp, the spooky season itself, and pointed it like a watering can at the ground under her feet 🎃💧. You do not need a gala or a movie career to run the same play. Plant one thing where people can see it. Clean one corner nobody pays you to clean. Throw one party that leaves the neighbourhood better than you found it, and throw it in costume 📣🌱. The gardens outlive the gala. The trees outlive the headlines. Forward is always a choice, and that means YOU too!

🛠️🌿 The Deep Dive: 5 Things Most Pro Landscapers Won't Tell You 🌿🛠️

1. Clear light snow with a leaf blower before boots and tires bond it into ice 🍂❄️
Today's snow, about 4.8 cm of it, is right in blower territory while it is fresh and dry: a leaf blower strips walks, steps, decks, and parked cars in minutes, long before it melts into the surface. The real enemy is not the snow, it is the traffic: every bootprint and tire track compresses fresh snow into a bonded ice skin that Thursday night's -9°C will set like concrete. Blow it off before anybody walks on it and you skip the chipping, the heavy salting, and the slip claims all in one pass. Save the shovel for the wet, heavy dumps, this first one is a wind job.

2. Take the tree-watering bag off before freeze-up, or the ice will take it off for you 🌳🧊
Slow-release watering bags, the zip-up gator style collars, did their job all summer, but any water left sitting in one tonight freezes solid against the trunk. Ice expansion splits the bag's seams, and the trapped moisture held against bark all winter invites rot and freeze damage exactly where a young tree can least afford it. Drain it, unzip it, and store it dry indoors; the tree is going dormant and its next real drink is a chinook thaw day, delivered by you with a hose, not by a bag of ice. Two minutes now beats replanting in May.

3. Salt by pavement temperature, not air temperature: rock salt quits near -9°C 🧂🌡️
Air temperature is the number on your phone; pavement temperature is the number that decides whether your de-icer works. A cheap infrared thermometer tells the truth: sun-hit concrete can run 5 to 10 degrees warmer than the air, while shaded north steps stay frozen solid on a -1°C day, which is why the same handful of salt melts one walk and does nothing on the next. Rock salt, sodium chloride, also has a practical floor around -9°C pavement temperature: below that it barely melts anything, and tonight's low of -4°C sliding to -9°C tomorrow night is exactly that line. Salt where the pavement is near the melting point, and switch to sand or grit for pure traction where it is colder.

4. Roof-rake the eaves today while the first snow is still light enough to move 🏠❄️
Ice dams are built one snowfall at a time, and the first layer is the easiest one you will ever move. While today's snow is fresh, pull it off the lowest metre or so of roof above the eaves with a roof rake, working from the ground, pulling down and toward you, never up under the shingles. Leave a thin skin of snow, a few centimetres, so the rake never scrapes the shingle surface itself. Every centimetre you clear today is meltwater that cannot pool behind an ice ridge in February and start hunting for a way into the attic.

5. Clear the street catch basin in front of the property before tonight's freeze locks it 🌧️🧹
The grate in the curb in front of a house is the whole street's escape hatch, and right now it is wearing a hat of wet leaves. Today's melt has to go somewhere: if the basin is plugged, the water ponds at the curb, spreads across the sidewalk, and tonight's freeze turns it into a skating rink with your address on it. Clear it from the curb side with a rake or a gloved hand, never stepping out into traffic, and pull the leaf mat back onto the boulevard to compost down. It is two minutes of work the city will never thank you for, and it quietly prevents the worst sheet of ice on the block.

🎶 Ron Hairemy's Songs That Guarantee LAWNgevity

Canadian Track'O'The Day: "Devil in a Midnight Mass" by Billy Talent (clean ✅). Billy Talent of Mississauga and Streetsville, Ontario, four school friends who became one of the biggest rock bands this country has produced, Juno winners many times over 🇨🇦🎸. This is the opening track of Billy Talent II from 2006, a gothic murder story set at the altar, all wiry guitar and Benjamin Kowalewicz spitting the verses like a man testifying. For me it is the perfect Canadian entry for Halloween week: church bells, bad deeds, and consequences arriving right on schedule ⛪🔔. Play it on the drive to the first snow route of the season and try not to drum on the steering wheel ❄️.

Today's Musical Funny-Bone: "Scary Ned" by Arrogant Worms (clean ✅). The Arrogant Worms of Kingston, Ontario, Canada's comedy institution since 1991, three decades of turning this country's quirks into singalongs 🇨🇦🎤. "Scary Ned" is their Halloween entry: a story song about the neighbourhood's resident bogeyman, the man every kid on the block was quietly terrified of, told with the Worms' trademark mix of affection and absurdity 👻. For me this one is pure trick-or-treat energy, the funny kind of scared, the kind with a candy bar at the end of it 🍬. Play it while the candy bowl gets stocked on Saturday.

Hair Whippin', Head Bangin', Finger Blazin' Therapy: "Entombment of a Machine" by Job For A Cowboy (clean per Spotify ✅, though the delivery is pure death metal violence, so sensitive ears have been warned). Job For A Cowboy of Glendale, Arizona, who broke out of the MySpace era and helped define deathcore with the Doom EP in 2005 🤘💀. "Entombment of a Machine" is the track that carried that EP everywhere: pig squeals, machine-gun drumming, and a title straight out of a nightmare about end-of-life machinery ⚙️😱. For me this is the heaviest three minutes on today's list by a wide and cheerful margin. Play it when the snow route stops being funny and starts being personal ❄️.

Tongue Trippin', Bar Spittin', Beat Killin' Bars: "A Nightmare on My Street" by DJ Jazzy Jeff & The Fresh Prince (clean ✅). Philadelphia, 1988, from the double album He's the DJ, I'm the Rapper, and still the greatest Halloween rap record ever pressed 🎤🔪. Will Smith spins a full Freddy Krueger story in character, funny and genuinely spooky at once, and the song was such a faithful nightmare that New Line Cinema sued over it and the original video ended up shelved with a disclaimer added to later pressings 🎬. For me this is proof that horror and humour are cousins: the scariest verse on today's list is also the one making me grin. Play it with the porch light on, obviously 🎃.

Māori Track: "Rū Ana Te Whenua" by Alien Weaponry (clean ✅). Alien Weaponry of Waipū, Northland, the de Jong brothers, Lewis, Henry, and Ethan, carrying te reo Māori to festival main stages around the world 🇳🇿🤘. "Rū Ana Te Whenua", the ground shakes, comes from their 2018 debut album Tū and tells the story of the Battle of Gate Pā, Pukehinahina, in 1864, where Māori defenders outfought a far larger British force 🌋. Sung in te reo, it is history with its boots on: for me, this is the most powerful four minutes on today's list, a reminder that the ground remembers everything done on it. Play it loud enough to feel the title happen 🥁.

Ron Hairemy Spotlight: "Brave", "Murder", and "Day" by Katatonia ft. Mikael Åkerfeldt (all clean per Spotify ✅, harsh vocals throughout the first two, so consider the congregation warned). Katatonia of Stockholm, Sweden, and these are the three opening tracks of Brave Murder Day from 1996, a single three-part suite that helped invent an entire mood of doom-laden death metal 🌑🎸. The voice doing the growling is a young Mikael Åkerfeldt, borrowed from Opeth because Katatonia's own Jonas Renkse could no longer growl and had moved behind the kit and the clean microphone. I picked this one myself, crew, and I have featured it once before, back on the old blog: some records earn a second hearing the way some graves earn a second visit 👻. Åkerfeldt is, in my words, a god of the night song, and this is him before the crown, already terrifying. Play the three tracks in order, no skipping, as the snow comes down 🌨️.

That's the goal. That's LAWNgevity. 🌱🔥

🌅 Conclusion

Bette Midler threw a Halloween party and a city's gardens lived because of it, which tells you a costume and a cause can share the same pair of shoulders 🎃🌱. The yard version is smaller and it rhymes: blow the light snow off before boots bond it down, get the watering bags off the young trees, salt by pavement temperature, rake the eaves, and free the catch basin before tonight's freeze does its paperwork ❄️.
