💌 Today's Message: The Day That Refused to Be Unlucky 🏍️

Some dates get a reputation they never earned. 🗓️ Friday the 13th has been scaring people for centuries, there is an actual clinical word for it, triskaidekaphobia, hotels quietly skip the thirteenth floor, and half the world would rather stay home than test the day. 😱 But in Port Dover, Ontario, a little fishing town of about 6,000 people on the north shore of Lake Erie, one man looked at the scariest square on the calendar and decided it did not deserve fear. It deserved a party. 🎉

On Friday, November 13, 1981, bike shop owner Chris Simons got about 25 of his friends together at the Commercial Hotel, the spot everybody called The Zoo, now Angelo's of Dover, just to celebrate being bikers on the day everybody else was hiding from. 🍻 They had such a good time that they made a pact on the spot: same town, same bar, every Friday the 13th from then on, no matter the season, no matter the weather. 🤝 No advertising. No posters. No committee, at first. Just word of mouth and a standing appointment with bad luck itself. 🏍️

Then the arithmetic lost its mind. 📈 When the crowds were still only 600 to 800 riders, Simons sat on the board of trade and told them he could see 100,000 people coming, and they laughed at him. By 2008, Norfolk County's own count had more than 100,000 people and over 12,000 motorcycles inside a town of about 6,000, and police estimates run as high as 140,000 on a warm one. 🚓 It became Canada's largest one-day motorcycle event, and the day even took a run at a Guinness record for the largest one-day rally on earth. 🌎 And the pact holds in February, too: when Friday the 13th lands in the dead of winter, the riders still come, through snow, because the appointment was never with the weather. It was with the day. ❄️

Next Friday, November 13, exactly one week from today, it fires again. 🔥 Forty-five years after 25 friends refused to be scared of a number, a whole town shuts its streets to cars and hands itself over to the people who showed up. That is the whole secret, crew: luck is just a story somebody tells about a date, and stories can be rewritten by anybody stubborn enough to make a standing appointment with the thing that scares them. 💪 Blog #13 landing on a Friday is not an omen, and neither is yours, whatever yours is this week, the bill, the diagnosis, the empty chair at the table. Meet it in the middle of town, with your people, on purpose. 🏴 Forward is always a choice, and that means YOU too!

Saturday a black cat crosses the whole province 🐈‍⬛: sunny, west winds at 29 km/h, and not one bad thing happens to anybody, which is the cat's whole defence. 🛠️🌿 The Deep Dive: 5 Things Most Pro Landscapers Won't Tell You 🌿🛠️

1. Watering at Noon Does NOT Burn Your Lawn 💧
The story goes that water droplets act like tiny magnifying glasses and scorch the grass, and it fails basic physics: a droplet sitting on a blade holds its focal point inside or behind itself, not on the tissue, and researchers who actually tested sunlit wet leaves found lawn grasses do not burn this way. The real cost of noon watering is evaporation and wind drift, which can waste a third of the water on a hot, windy day. Morning watering is about efficiency, not burn prevention. And if the lawn is wilting at 2pm, water it then: a thirsty lawn suffers more than a wet one ever will.

2. Grass Clippings Do NOT Cause Thatch 🍂
Thatch is a dense mat of stems, roots, and crowns, the slow-decaying structural parts of the plant. Clippings are none of that: they are roughly 80 percent water, they break down in a week or two, and they hand back up to a quarter of the nitrogen your lawn needs, free. Bagging every pass starves the turf and landfills fertility you already paid for. Real thatch builds when heavy fertilizing and shallow daily watering push roots up to the surface. Mulch-mow without guilt, and only reach for the core aerator when the thatch layer itself passes about a centimetre and a half.

3. Planting by the Moon Is a Calendar, Not a Spell 🌙
Farmers have planted by moon signs for centuries, and it is a beautiful tradition with one problem: controlled trials keep finding no reliable effect of lunar phase on germination or yield. The moon's gravitational pull on a seed is trivial next to the fence post beside it. What actually decides a planting is soil temperature, soil moisture, and your frost dates, all of which you can measure with a thermometer and a knuckle. Keep the almanac, honestly, it is a good read. Just use it for the frost charts, and let the soil cast the deciding vote.

4. Beer, Pop, and Dish Soap Are NOT Lawn Food 🍺
The famous pantry tonics promise a golf course out of the grocery bag, and the chemistry is not there: beer and pop are sugar water, a light snack for microbes and zero meaningful nitrogen, phosphorus, or potassium. Dish soap is worse than useless in quantity, stripping the waxy coating off blades, stressing the turf, and doing the soil biology no favours, and the old recipes that lean on ammonia are just a sloppy, risky way to buy nitrogen. A soil test and the right fertilizer will beat the entire pantry, cheaper, and without giving your lawn a hangover.

5. Coffee Grounds Will NOT Sour Your Soil ☕
Here is the part nobody tells you: the acid in coffee is water-soluble, which means it ends up in your cup, not in the grounds. Used grounds test close to neutral, around pH 6.5, so spread over Edmonton's alkaline clay they will not budge the pH, and they will not fix iron chlorosis either, because that yellowing is iron locked out by high pH, a job for chelated iron and sulfur, not breakfast. Grounds still earn their keep as a thin ingredient in the compost, where they belong. Laid on thick as a mulch they mat down, repel water, and can stall seedlings, so compost them, don't prescribe them.

🇨🇦 Canadian Track'O'The Day: The Toronto Song by Three Dead Trolls in a Baggie (clean). My own people, an Edmonton comedy troupe formed in 1987 by Wes Borg, Joe Bird, and Neil Grahn, declaring cheerful war on the centre of the universe. It comes from Con Troupo Comedius, also on the Skit Happens collection, and it has been misattributed to the Arrogant Worms for three decades, which the Trolls have always found hilarious. It even anchored the 2007 mockumentary Let's All Hate Toronto, where Borg toured the country collecting grievances. No malice in it, just playful insults, civic pride, and a hook. Play it any time you need to remember that Edmonton humour hits harder because it has to shovel its way out first. His link: https://youtu.be/iRjurRdep_Q

🤪 Today's Musical Funny-Bone: Welcome to the Internet by Bo Burnham [E]. From Inside (2021), the special Burnham wrote, shot, lit, and edited alone in one room during lockdown, and the album Inside (The Songs), released June 10, 2021. This track is a carnival barker villain number where the internet itself offers you everything, all of the time, with a smile that should terrify you. It runs past 90 million views on YouTube, and the profanity and adult references run wall to wall, so mind the audience. Play it the next time you catch yourself doomscrolling at midnight and need the spiral named out loud, in rhyming couplets, by a man in a spotlight.

🤘 Hair Whippin', Head Bangin', Finger Blazin' Therapy: Tunak Tunak Tun by Bloodywood (clean). New Delhi folk metal, and the single greatest genre swerve on today's shelf. Bloodywood started in 2016 with Karan Katiyar and Jayant Bhadula making metal covers of pop songs, and this is their 2018 metal rebuild of Daler Mehndi's 1998 bhangra anthem, released as Tunak Tunak Metal with guest vocals from Bonde do Metaleiro: dhol drums, tumbi twang, and drop-tuned guitars under the most indestructible dance hook India ever produced. The original was the first Indian music video shot on green screen and went on to become a planetary meme, and Bloodywood went the other direction entirely, becoming the first Indian metal band to chart on Billboard with their own album Rakshak. Play it when the crew's morale needs airdropping: nobody stays slumped through a bhangra breakdown. Nobody.

🎤 Tongue Trippin', Bar Spittin, Beat Killin Bars: Couch Potato by "Weird Al" Yankovic (clean). From Poodle Hat (2003), a full-throttle parody of Eminem's Lose Yourself with the flow rebuilt around channel surfing, reality TV, and a man dissolving into his own recliner. Al was on repeat through my whole childhood, Alapalooza was the first CD I ever bought with my own money, and when I saw him live at Capital Ex he opened the show with Canadian Idiot, which felt personal. Eminem allowed the parody but refused a music video, so the song lives exactly where it belongs: in the car, at full volume. Play it on the drive between jobs when the day needs its edges sanded down by a genius in a Hawaiian shirt.

🌿 Māori Track: Tangaroa Whakamautai by Maisey Rika (clean). From Whitiora (2012), Rika's landmark album recorded entirely in te reo Māori, which reached number 9 on the New Zealand singles chart in a mainstream market that rarely makes room for that, and this title track took home Best Māori Song and Best Music Video at the 2013 Waiata Māori Music Awards. Rika is of Ngāti Awa, Tūhoe, Te Arawa, and Te Whānau-a-Apanui descent, an Arts Foundation Laureate, and here she sings to Tangaroa, god of the sea, with taonga pūoro and strings under her voice. The words speak of the kaitiaki of the ocean, and I will let you work out why this one sits close to my chest. Play it at golden hour with water in sight, or when you need to remember what your name means.

🔦 Ron Hairemy Spotlight: I'll Be Fine by DIZZYISDEAD [E]. A single from January 10, 2025, on Regime Music Group, from Blake Lounsbury, a Canadian alternative artist out of Woodstock, Ontario, who has stacked more than 40 million streams writing sad-pop confessionals for the kids who feel everything at full volume. The hook is a promise somebody is trying to believe: yeah, I'm fucked up, but I'll be fine. Full disclosure from my own notebook, this song is sitting in the to-learn pile of my guitar songbook, which tells you exactly how deep the hook is set. This one does not get a when-to-listen cue, because it is not that kind of song: it is a say-it-until-it-is-true song, and a kid from Woodstock built 40 million streams out of saying it on his worst nights first. That is not a playlist closer. That is the whole point of the shelf.
